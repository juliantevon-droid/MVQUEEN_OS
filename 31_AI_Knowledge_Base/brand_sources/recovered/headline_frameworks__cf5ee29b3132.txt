# 📰 MVQUEEN — Headline Frameworks

---

## What a MVQUEEN Headline Does

A headline carries two jobs simultaneously:
1. Make her stop and read
2. Set the emotional atmosphere for everything that follows

MVQUEEN headlines are never screaming for attention.
They draw it — the way a beautifully lit room draws you in before you understand why.

**The standard:** Every headline should work as a standalone sentence.
If it only makes sense with what comes after it, it's not strong enough.

---

## The 10 MVQUEEN Headline Frameworks

---

### Framework 01 — The Doctrine Statement
A brand truth stated with quiet authority.
Works for: Brand content, editorial, campaigns, email subjects.

**Structure:** `[Brand belief stated as fact]`

**Examples:**
- *Softness is not something you outgrow.*
- *Luxury was always meant to feel like this.*
- *The right ritual changes everything.*
- *Beauty should restore you — not require you.*
- *Femininity is presence, not performance.*

---

### Framework 02 — The Transformation Promise
Names the specific before → after without being salesy.
Works for: Product pages, ads, email campaigns, landing pages.

**Structure:** `[Before state] → [After state]` or `From ___ to ___`

**Examples:**
- *From routine to ritual.*
- *From surviving the morning to owning it.*
- *Skin that went from dry and tired to soft and alive.*
- *The shift from doing beauty to feeling it.*

---

### Framework 03 — The Woman-First Headline
Centers her identity — not the product.
Works for: Ads, social, email, product collections.

**Structure:** `For the woman who ___` / `Made for ___`

**Examples:**
- *For the woman who chooses softness on purpose.*
- *Made for women who feel everything.*
- *For her. The one who's been waiting for this.*
- *For the version of you who takes her time.*

---

### Framework 04 — The Sensory Lead
Opens with a physical or atmospheric feeling.
Works for: Product descriptions, editorial, campaigns, social.

**Structure:** `[Sensory experience as opening]`

**Examples:**
- *Skin that drinks it in.*
- *The weight of real silk. The warmth of intention.*
- *Softness you can feel from across the room.*
- *The scent of an unhurried morning.*
- *Light that flatters everything it touches.*

---

### Framework 05 — The Quiet Disruption
Challenges an assumption without aggression.
Works for: Brand content, social, ads, email.

**Structure:** `[What people assume] — [What we know instead]`

**Examples:**
- *Luxury doesn't have to feel out of reach.*
- *Self-care was never supposed to feel like work.*
- *You were never supposed to earn the right to feel beautiful.*
- *Femininity was never the problem.*

---

### Framework 06 — The Named Product Headline
Leads with the product but wrapped in meaning.
Works for: Product launches, announcements, collection reveals.

**Structure:** `Introducing [Product] — [What it means]`

**Examples:**
- *Introducing [Product Name] — made for your softest mornings.*
- *[Product Name]. For women who know what they want.*
- *Meet [Product Name]. She's been a while coming.*
- *[Product Name] — the ritual you didn't know was missing.*

---

### Framework 07 — The Question Headline
Asks the question she's already asking herself.
Works for: Blog articles, social, email subjects, ads.

**Structure:** `[Question that names her exact feeling or desire]`

**Examples:**
- *What if your morning routine felt like the best part of your day?*
- *What does luxury actually feel like when it was made for you?*
- *Have you ever felt beautiful just sitting still?*
- *What would change if beauty restored you instead of exhausted you?*

---

### Framework 08 — The Number Headline
Specific numbers signal authority and value.
Works for: Blog articles, carousels, email, educational content.

**Structure:** `[Number] + [specific promise]`

**Examples:**
- *5 rituals that changed how mornings feel.*
- *The one product my shelf hasn't been without.*
- *3 signs your beauty routine needs to become a ritual.*
- *7 things every intentional woman keeps in her space.*

---

### Framework 09 — The Permission Headline
Gives her permission she didn't realize she needed.
Works for: Brand content, campaigns, social, email.

**Structure:** `You're allowed to ___`

**Examples:**
- *You're allowed to want things that feel this good.*
- *You're allowed to take up space softly.*
- *You're allowed to choose luxury and not justify it.*
- *You're allowed to make this about you.*

---

### Framework 10 — The Poetic One-Liner
A single line with rhythm and meaning.
Works for: Campaign headlines, brand statements, social captions, packaging.

**Structure:** `[Short, rhythmic, emotionally resonant line]`

**Examples:**
- *Soft. Intentional. Yours.*
- *Beauty that belongs to you.*
- *The world gets louder. She gets softer.*
- *Made with intention. Worn with presence.*
- *Not just products. A whole philosophy.*

## Headline Length by Format

| Format | Ideal Length | Notes |
|--------|-------------|-------|
| Email subject line | 5-8 words | Under 50 characters for mobile |
| Social caption headline | 5-12 words | First line before "more" cutoff |
| Blog article H1 | 8-14 words | SEO-aware, brand voice first |
| Ad headline | 4-8 words | Punch and clarity |
| Product page headline | 3-8 words | Evocative, brand-aligned |
| Campaign headline | 3-6 words | Iconic, standalone |
| SMS opening | Under 10 words | Entire message is the headline |

---

## Headline Quality Test

Before using any headline, ask:

1. **Does it create a feeling in under 5 seconds?**
2. **Could any other brand write this?** (If yes — it's not MVQUEEN enough)
3. **Does it honor the woman it's speaking to?**
4. **Is every word earning its place?**
5. **Does it make you want to read what comes next?**

Fail any of these — rewrite before publishing.
